## Overview

A community platform designed to help creators build and nurture their audiences through meaningful interactions, not just content consumption.

## The Problem

Existing platforms optimize for:
- Time spent (addiction)
- Ad impressions (interruption)
- Viral content (outrage)

We wanted to optimize for:
- **Meaningful connections**
- **Knowledge sharing**
- **Community health**

## My Approach

### Research Phase

Spent 3 months studying successful communities:
- 100+ community manager interviews
- Analyzed 50 thriving Discord servers
- Studied academic research on social dynamics

### Key Insights

1. **Small groups outperform large ones** for engagement
2. **Rituals create belonging** (weekly events, traditions)
3. **Recognition drives contribution** (badges, leaderboards)
4. **Moderation is community care** (not censorship)

## Features We Built

### Circles (Small Groups)
- Auto-match members with similar interests
- 8-12 person groups for deeper discussions
- Rotating membership to meet new people

### Community Rituals
- Scheduled events with reminders
- Recurring discussion threads
- Anniversary and milestone celebrations

### Recognition System
- Contribution tracking
- Peer endorsements
- Custom badges

## Growth Strategy

We grew from 0 to 50,000 users through:

1. **Creator partnerships** - Onboarded 100 creators with existing audiences
2. **Community-led growth** - Members invite members
3. **Content marketing** - Blog posts on community building

## Metrics That Matter

We intentionally avoided vanity metrics:

| What We Track | What We Don't |
|---------------|---------------|
| Messages per member | Total messages |
| Reply rate | Views |
| Member retention | Sign-ups |
| NPS score | DAU/MAU |

## Results

- **68% monthly retention** (vs industry average of 25%)
- **NPS of 72** (world-class)
- **4.2 messages per member per day**
- Acquired by larger platform in 2024

## Reflections

Building for community health instead of engagement metrics was the right call. It required patience—growth was slower initially—but the communities we built were genuinely valuable to members.
